American Magnetics Model 430 programmer (and the older Model 420) for superconducting
magnets. The magnet's configuration (coil constant, current limit, ramp rates, the
persistent switch's heater current and times, quench detection) is set once on the
programmer's front panel. The driver only reads it. What the driver does is make sure
the magnet is driven safely.

**Address.** Model 430 over Ethernet: `TCP::<ip address>::7180`, or the RS-232 port.
Model 420: its RS-232 port, e.g. `COM3`. The programmer's field units (kG or T) and
ramp-rate units are read and converted; `field` is always in T and `ramp_rate` in T/min.

## Setting the field

Setting `field` starts the ramp and returns at once, like pressing RAMP on the front
panel. `status` reads `RAMPING`, then `HOLDING`. A sweep that steps the field waits at
every step with a [point action](../../guide/point-actions.md):

```python
wait_until(lambda: get('magnet.status') == 'HOLDING', timeout=3600)
```

## Persistent switch

`IsPersistentSwitchInstalled` (a setting, `Yes` / `No`) says whether the magnet has a
persistent switch. Option `auto` (the default) takes it from the programmer's
configuration. With `Yes`:

* the field can only be changed in **driven** mode (switch heater on). In persistent
  mode, setting `field` is refused: the supply would only change the current in its
  leads, while the magnet keeps its field;
* **Enter Persistent** (`enter_persistent`) and **Exit Persistent** (`exit_persistent`)
  switch between the two modes.

Each action does its steps in the order that cannot quench the magnet. Before every
step it reads the programmer's state again:

| Enter Persistent | Exit Persistent |
|---|---|
| 1. The supply must hold the field (`HOLDING`). Wait `settle_time` seconds, and until the magnet voltage is below `max_magnet_voltage`. | 1. Ramp the supply to the magnet's persistent current, and wait until it holds there. |
| 2. Turn the switch heater off, and wait while the switch cools (`COOLING SWITCH`). | 2. Heat the switch, **only if** the supply current matches the magnet current within `match_tolerance` A. |
| 3. Ramp the leads to zero (option `zero_leads`), and wait for `AT ZERO`. Status: `PERSISTENT`. | 3. Wait while the switch heats (`HEATING SWITCH`), then hold the field. Status: `HOLDING`. |

Heating the switch at the wrong supply current forces the difference into the magnet
at once, which can quench it. That is why exiting never heats the switch before the
currents match.

An action starts the procedure and returns. The procedure then goes on by itself (the
backend checks the magnet every `poll_interval` seconds), and you can watch the field
and the status meanwhile. `status` reads `ENTERING PERSISTENT` or `EXITING PERSISTENT`
until it is done, and `operation` shows the current step. A script waits for it:

```python
set('magnet.enter_persistent')
wait_until(lambda: get('magnet.status') == 'PERSISTENT', timeout=3600)
```

If a step does not finish in time, or the programmer does something unexpected (paused
from the front panel, a ramp refused …), the procedure stops in a safe state, without
heating the switch. `operation` then says why. The `pause` action stops the supply and
the procedure. Field writes and the other action are refused while a procedure runs,
and while the switch is heating or cooling.

## Quench

The programmer detects a quench itself and takes the supply to zero. The driver checks
for one at every call, and the backend checks every `poll_interval` seconds, even when
nothing reads the magnet. When a quench is detected:

* the running sweep or script is aborted (its data file says why), the run queue stops,
  and running user actions are aborted;
* every front end shows the fault, and the instrument's last error reads `FAULT: …`;
* until the quench is cleared, field writes and the persistent actions are refused.
  Reading the field still works, so you can watch it decay.

Clear it with the `clear_quench` action (or RESET QUENCH on the front panel) once the
magnet is safe. Then start the queue again. See
[Robustness](../../guide/robustness.md#safety-faults).
